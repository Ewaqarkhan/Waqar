Research

At the intersection of deep learning and network security, my work asks:

  • Can VAE-LSTM and Transformers surface intrusions in continuous traffic that signature-based IDS miss?
  • How do detectors degrade under concept drift, missing signals, and non-IID data — and can federated / split learning stay robust?
  • Can SHAP-style explainability and TinyML quantisation make these models both interpretable for operators and deployable on IoT hardware?

Publications

Few-Shot Learning for EEG-Based Diagnosis of Alzheimer’s and Frontotemporal DementiaUnder review

Khan, W.

Proposes a few-shot learning approach to diagnose neurodegenerative diseases from EEG signals, tackling the data-scarcity problem that limits clinical deployment of deep diagnostic models.

Published & accepted

Federated Learning-Based NIDS for Smart Grid: The Impact of Feature Selection and Model SizeAccepted

Khan, W., et al. · IEEE AIoT Conference, 2026

Investigates how feature-selection strategies and model-size constraints interact in federated NIDS deployed across distributed smart-grid environments, where data cannot be centralised due to privacy and bandwidth limits.

A Novel Transformer-Based Explainable AI Approach Using SHAP for Intrusion Detection in VANETsPublished

Khan, W., et al. · Computer Networks (Elsevier), 2025

Introduces a transformer architecture for intrusion detection in Vehicular Ad-hoc Networks and couples it with SHAP-based explainability so analysts can interpret which traffic features drove each alert — a key requirement for trustworthy deployment in safety-critical transportation systems.

Enhancing Security in 6G-Enabled Wireless Sensor Networks for Smart CitiesPublished

Khan, W., et al. · Frontiers in Sustainable Cities, 2025

Presents a multi-deep-learning intrusion detection approach for 6G-enabled wireless sensor networks supporting smart-city infrastructure, analysing time-series data flows to flag anomalous behaviour that conventional rule-based systems miss.

Explainable and Optimized Hybrid Deep Learning Model for Early Diagnosis of Alzheimer’s and Frontotemporal DementiaPublished

Khan, W., et al. · Frontiers in Medicine, 2025

Develops a hybrid deep-learning model optimised for early diagnosis of Alzheimer’s and Frontotemporal dementia, paired with explainability to support clinician trust in model predictions during the critical early-screening stage.
