About Me

Hi! I am Waqar Khan. I am a Machine Learning researcher and Cybersecurity specialist with an M.Sc. in Cybersecurity from the National University of Sciences & Technology (NUST), Pakistan, and six peer-reviewed publications in high-impact venues. I specialize in developing AI-driven intrusion detection systems (NIDS), time-series anomaly detection, and Explainable AI to help human analysts make transparent, trustworthy decisions. I am highly experienced in Python, PyTorch, and deploying lightweight models on constrained IoT hardware.

I am currently seeking a PhD-Researcher and Lecturer position to apply my empirical ML expertise and contribute to both the research and educational missions of an academic department. If you are recruiting PhD students or have an open academic position, please get in touch — I would be glad to share my research vision and CV.

Seeking PhD / Lecturer opportunities for 2026. Open to research-focused roles in machine learning for cybersecurity, time-series anomaly detection, and explainable AI. Email: waqar.khance@gmail.com.

My research explores the intersection of deep learning and network security, asking practical questions such as:

  • How can deep architectures such as VAE-LSTM and Transformers reconstruct temporal patterns in continuous traffic streams to surface intrusions that signature-based IDS miss?
  • How do learning-based detectors degrade under concept drift, missing signals, and non-IID data distributions — and how can federated and split-learning paradigms remain robust under these stresses?
  • How can Explainable AI (e.g., SHAP) translate opaque deep-model decisions into human-readable causes for security operators in SOCs and XDR pipelines?
  • How can TinyML and model quantization push capable intrusion-detection models onto resource-constrained IoT hardware without sacrificing accuracy?
